I am 28 and I have spent about ten years in manufacturing. This is how I got
from there to here.

## A kid with a computer

I had a computer from an early age and no money to spend on it. So I learned to
get by: pirated games, cracked software, whatever I could make run. I am not
going to pretend otherwise. Getting a crack to work, or tweaking a cheat script
until the game accepted it, was more interesting to me than the game itself.

I spent hours on forums and experimented with HTML. I wanted to know how servers
worked, and how one person could host a game like Tibia and have hundreds of
people playing on it. I never built anything real back then. But I have always
liked taking things apart to see how they work.

## The shop floor

At 18 I started at Markethouse as a carpenter's assistant. Manual work, building
displays and furniture for supermarkets. Over the years I moved into mechanical
design, and later into leading the design team.

## First attempt

In 2020 I decided to study computing seriously and enrolled in Computer Science
at a private university. Then the pandemic hit. Classes moved online and the
systems barely worked. At the same time supermarkets were booming, which meant
their suppliers were too, and that was us. Work took everything. I dropped out.

## Second attempt

In 2023 I tried again, and this time I planned it. I saved money, left my job so
I could study, and earned a place in Database Technology at FATEC, a public
college. Some time later Markethouse invited me back in a senior role, and I
accepted. I finished the degree in 2026.

## Now

I am starting a software career right after the AI boom, so the bar is higher
than it has ever been. My answer is to build and learn until the work speaks
for itself.

That is why I built a production planning system for Markethouse as a freelance
project, and why I did not build it alone. First I worked with my best friend, a
mid-level engineer, who helped get it off the ground. Then with my wife, a senior
engineer. Working next to people better than me is the fastest way I know to
learn. I am also about to start a mentorship with a very experienced professor.

## Away from the screen

I lift weights, and I take it seriously. After a day of doing math at work, I
need something physical. I spend time with my wife, our cats and our dogs.
Lately I have taken up bird watching: I get home, go out to the yard barefoot,
and watch them.
